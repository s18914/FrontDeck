Web accessibility means building sites that **people with disabilities can perceive, understand, navigate and operate**: blind users with screen readers, keyboard-only users, people with low vision, color blindness, hearing loss, or motor and cognitive impairments.

Why it matters:

- **Users** — roughly 1 in 6 people has a significant disability, and temporary or situational limits (a broken arm, bright sunlight, a noisy room) affect everyone.
- **Legal** — many countries require it. In the EU the **European Accessibility Act (EAA)** has applied since June 2025 to many private-sector services such as e-commerce and banking, and its technical benchmark builds on **WCAG** level AA.
- **Quality** — accessible markup tends to improve SEO, usability on mobile, and testability (Testing Library queries elements by role and name).

The cheapest approach is **semantic HTML** and keyboard support from day one, not patching right before release.
